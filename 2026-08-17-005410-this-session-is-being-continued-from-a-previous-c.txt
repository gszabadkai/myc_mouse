⏺ This is a real tension worth resolving before the cut, and the data resolves
  it cleanly. Let me lay out what I verified.

  First, the convention check — timepoint_neg NES for MITOCARTA_OXPHOS_SUBUNITS
  is −2.670 and timepoint_pos is −2.715, which matches the corpus's recorded
  WT-vs-Myc+ OXPHOS pair exactly. So the sign convention is confirmed: negative
  = lower at 12W.

  The short answer: the two facts are not in tension — one entails the other

  1. NES is scale-free by construction, so it cannot see the fade where you 
  would look for it.

  fGSEA ranks genes on the Wald statistic and normalises each ES against a
  permutation null built from that same ranked list. Multiply every gene's
  effect by 0.5 and the rank order is unchanged, the null is rebuilt from the
  same list, and the NES is unchanged. Measured:

  ┌──────────────────────────────────────┬────────┬────────────────────┐
  │                                      │   6W   │        12W         │
  ├──────────────────────────────────────┼────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ median |LFC|, the 1,967 Myc-DE genes │ 0.678  │ 0.311 (ratio 0.46) │
  ├──────────────────────────────────────┼────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ median lfcSE, same genes             │ 0.189  │ 0.193              │
  ├──────────────────────────────────────┼────────┼────────────────────┤
  │ median NES, 866 sets                 │ +1.768 │ +2.008             │
  └──────────────────────────────────────┴────────┴────────────────────┘

  The amplitude halves, the standard errors do not move (so it is not a power
  difference), and the NES rises. fig1_nes_preserved_sharpened.R already
  documents why it rises (flatter 12W list → smaller normaliser).

  So the fade has to surface somewhere else — and the only contrasts that can
  show it are the ones spanning the two ages, which is exactly what's in
  outputs/fgsea/.

  2. Rank stability predicts the negative Myc+ timeline; it does not forbid it.

  6>12W_myc = 6>12W_wt + interaction, and interaction = myc_12W − myc_6W. If the
  Myc effect keeps its shape and loses half its amplitude, then the interaction
  is a near-perfect negative copy of the Myc programme. Measured across 866
  sets:

  - Spearman(myc_6W, interaction) = −0.878, slope −0.718
  - Spearman(myc_6W, timepoint_pos) = −0.838

  Within Myc+ animals, the genes Myc pushed up at 6W come back down by 12W — in
  the same order they went up. That is the single most enriched thing fGSEA can
  report. A rank-unstable fade would give a weak, incoherent temporal NES; the
  fact that timepoint_pos is a clean mirror image is itself further evidence the
  programme kept its shape.

  3. The WT timeline is a different question, and it is not the Myc programme at
  all.

  6>12W_wt contains no Myc. Its relation to the Myc effect is ~zero:
  Spearman(myc_6W, timepoint_neg) = −0.068. And that near-zero is an average
  over two opposite families:

  ┌─────────────────────────────┬─────┬───────────────────────┬─────────────┐
  │                             │  n  │   median NES in WT    │   rho to    │
  │                             │     │       timeline        │   myc_6W    │
  ├─────────────────────────────┼─────┼───────────────────────┼─────────────┤
  │ Myc-induced sets (myc_6W >  │ 579 │ −1.16                 │ −0.314      │
  │ 0)                          │     │                       │             │
  ├─────────────────────────────┼─────┼───────────────────────┼─────────────┤
  │ Myc-repressed sets (myc_6W  │ 287 │ −1.28                 │ +0.345      │
  │ < 0)                        │     │                       │             │
  └─────────────────────────────┴─────┴───────────────────────┴─────────────┘

  The WT gland goes down on both families. Myc reversing would push them in
  opposite directions. This is the gland maturing — its top negatives are
  MG_STEM_GARCIASOLA −3.05, MG_ALV_C4 −3.05, MG_TEB_VS_DUCTAL_HS_GRAY_UP −2.76.

  4. "Most pathways negative" is a statement about the library, not the 
  transcriptome.

  At gene level timepoint_neg is essentially balanced — 47.6% of genes negative,
  median stat +0.085, and still 50.9% among the top expression quartile. Yet
  73.9% of NES are negative. The negativity comes from what this library 
  contains: 605 of 866 sets are TF-target + mammary-development lanes, and those
  are the coherent programmes that switch off with maturation. By category the
  WT timeline is not uniformly negative:

  tf_targets       -1.46 (421)      mitocarta               +0.81 (65)
  mammary_dev      -1.27 (184)      metabolism              +0.88 (71)
  hallmark         -1.23 (50)       biogenesis_discrim      +0.80 (16)
  myc_signatures   -1.21 (16)       biog x apoptosis        +1.00 (8)

  The mitochondrial compartment as a whole rises in the maturing wild-type
  gland. It is specifically the OXPHOS arm that falls (OXPHOS_SUBUNITS −2.67) —
  the substrate half of your closing claim, and consistent with
  HALLMARK_E2F_TARGETS sitting at +0.60 (ns) in the WT timeline while it is
  −1.73 in the Myc+ one: the gland de-respires without de-proliferating.

  Two consequences for the text

  (a) "The ranking did not change" is a pathway-level statement and must say so.
  Spearman 0.933 over 866 sets. At gene level it is much weaker: 0.724 on the
  Myc-DE genes, 0.520 over all 18,523. A reader who is not told will read
  "ranking" as genes, and the gene-level number does not support the sentence.

  (b) A temporal NES and a genotype NES are not on the same ruler. Each is
  normalised inside its own ranked list. HALLMARK_MYC_TARGETS_V1 at −2.17 in the
  Myc+ timeline and +2.86 in the 6W genotype contrast are not comparable
  magnitudes — only signs and within-contrast orderings are.

  (median NES −1.22 Myc+ / −1.13 WT). The current object is
  results/fgsea_percategory.rds (script 20, 866 sets), and it is the one that
  carries the interaction ranking — the contrast that isolates the fade, which
  script 04 never computed. Cite the newer one.

  The one misconception

  fGSEA does not compare two rankings. It takes one ranked gene list and asks
  where a set sits inside it. That's the whole source of the confusion — and
  there are three lists here, not two:

  ┌─────┬───────────┬───────────────────────────────────┐
  │     │   list    │            what it is             │
  ├─────┼───────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
  │ 1   │ myc_6W    │ Myc+ minus WT, at six weeks       │
  ├─────┼───────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
  │ 2   │ myc_12W   │ Myc+ minus WT, at twelve weeks    │
  ├─────┼───────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
  │ 3   │ 6>12W_myc │ 12W minus 6W, within Myc+ animals │
  └─────┴───────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘

  "The ranking is preserved" is a statement about lists 1 and 2. List 3 is built
  from the difference between them — which is precisely the part that was lost.
  A difference of two proportional things is not zero.

  Why halving must produce a negative timeline

  Take one Myc target: 2-fold over wild type at 6W, 1.4-fold at 12W. Inside the
  Myc+ animals, that gene went down. So did every other Myc target — in the same
  order. So list 3 is the negative of list 1, and the set that tops lists 1 and
  2 sits at the bottom of list 3.

  Two consequences that are worth sitting with:

  - NES is scale-free, so it cannot see the fade where you'd look for it.
  Multiply every effect by 0.5: the order is unchanged, and fGSEA rebuilds its
  null from the same list, so the score is unchanged. Measured — median |LFC|
  0.678 → 0.311 while median lfcSE goes 0.189 → 0.193 (so it isn't power), and
  median NES rises, +1.77 → +2.01. The fade is invisible in lists 1 and 2 by
  construction. It has to come out somewhere, and the only place left is a
  contrast that spans the ages.
  - So the negative timeline is not evidence against stability — it is what 
  stability produces. A rank-unstable fade would give a weak, incoherent
  timeline. A clean mirror is what a preserved shape looks like from the other
  side. Real data: Spearman(myc_6W, interaction) = −0.878, Spearman(myc_6W,
  6>12W_myc) = −0.838 over 866 sets.

  The figure

  outputs/figures/panels/explainer_nes_sign.pdf (89 × 112 mm), from
  figures/panels/explainer_nes_sign.R.

  - Top — the simulation as a slopegraph: 2,000 genes, a 120-gene target set,
  the 12W effect exactly 0.5× the 6W one, static wild type. No line crosses
  another (preserved ranking); every line moves toward zero (the fade). The line
  itself is the timeline contrast.
  - Middle — that same set through real fgsea::fgsea() three times: +3.57, 
  +3.58, −3.58. Same genes, same set. The ticks move from the left of the first
  two lists to the right of the third.
  - Bottom — real data, so the toy can be checked against what it explains.

  One thing the simulation exposed that I'd not have written down otherwise: the
  compression is toward zero, not downward. So the fade lifts the sets Myc
  represses — the pale lines below zero rise. The library confirms it: over the
  579 sets Myc induces the median Myc+ timeline NES is −1.80; over the 287 it
  represses, +1.03. A fade gives a sign flip relative to the Myc effect, never a
  uniform decline.

  Where the toy is simpler than the gland — and it matters for your cut

  The simulation has a static wild type. The real 6>12W_myc is 6>12W_wt plus the
  fade, and the bottom region draws the difference:

  - Hallmark MYC targets V1 — Myc+ timeline −2.17 vs wild-type −1.38. The fade
  dominates. The toy's case.
  - MitoCarta OXPHOS subunits — −2.72 vs −2.67. Identical. The fade contributes

  The organising idea

  Your original criterion — "which can be best quantified" — optimises for
  defensibility, and defensibility is bought with normalisation. Clarity is
  bought with the opposite thing: units the reader already owns. So the two
  criteria don't merely differ, they pull in a predictable direction, and the
  whole corpus sits on one side of it:

  ┌──────┬──────────────────┬─────────────────────────┬─────────────────────┐
  │ rung │    instrument    │       divided by        │ what the division   │
  │      │                  │                         │      destroys       │
  ├──────┼──────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────┤
  │      │ summed           │                         │ nothing beyond      │
  │ 0    │ normalised       │ library size            │ depth               │
  │      │ counts           │                         │                     │
  ├──────┼──────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────┤
  │ 1    │ % share          │ the transcriptome       │ magnitude           │
  ├──────┼──────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────┤
  │ 2    │ log2FC           │ the other group         │ level               │
  ├──────┼──────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────┤
  │ 3    │ mitoPPS          │ the rest of the         │ content             │

  ├──────┼──────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────┤

  ├──────┼──────────────────┼─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────┤

  └──────┴──────────────────┴─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────┘

  Your message needs rungs 0–2. The corpus drew rungs 3–5. And correcting that
  costs no rigour, because since scripts 21/34/36/40/43/45 the defence moved 
  from the normalisation to the null. A raw fold change with a matched-set null
  is now both more defensible and more legible than an NES. Both properties
  moved; only the panels didn't.

  Two immediate consequences: fGSEA earns exactly one panel (its contribution is
  a claim about method, and that needs making once — three NES panels persuade
  no more than one), and mitoPPS earns exactly one (reallocation is 1F's
  subject; elsewhere it pays the teaching cost without collecting the benefit).

  The highest-value single change

  Fig. 2I's x axis: mitoPPS score → absolute OXPHOS-subunit counts. The problem
  isn't aesthetic — the axis contradicts the sentence it supports. Your closing
  hypothesis says "high respiratory state", and the panel's own legend already
  has to warn that a high mitoPPS value means budget share, not more 
  respiration. Script 45 PART G has already computed the per-animal absolute
  levels.

  I've framed it as a test with a decision rule fixed in advance, not a fix — a
  ratio cancels the global common-mode axis and an absolute level doesn't, and
  script 36 showed the OXPHOS composite is that axis (r = 0.968). It may not
  reproduce. If it does, you gain a legible axis and independent corroboration
  on a second quantifier, which is exactly what a marginal result needs. If it
  doesn't, the ruler-dependence goes in the bounds. Either way it gets reported.

  Where the message is most exposed — and the fix makes it stronger

  "Unequivocally identified the apoptotic arm as the primary driver" cannot
  stand: the death composites are MitoCarta-defined, the priming interaction is
  null (int_p 0.177/0.263), and nothing attenuates more than expression-matched 
  genes (all BH p > 0.14) — so there is no arm to be primary.

  But what survives is sharper than what's claimed. Your strongest evidential
  move isn't a pathway result at all: you fitted one global rate to the
  transcriptome and asked which genes leave it. Two pre-specified genes went in;
  Bbc3 came out at the 0.51st percentile of 8,774, Bcl2l11 at the 91.6th, and
  Foxo3 at the 0.46th with four genes between them. That's a genome-wide test
  with a named prior, and reviewers trust it more than any enrichment score.
  Replace "the apoptotic arm" with "one apoptotic node, named in advance" —
  weaker-sounding, much stronger evidence — and build the section around 2H
  rather than putting it fourth.

  Seven sentences flagged

  The two most consequential:

  - "highly significant (p = 0.0052)" — that's the composition-adjusted term;
  against its own 5,000-permutation null it sits at the 91.7th percentile, 
  empirical p = 0.083, and adding a timepoint term takes it to 0.088. It's the
  one axis-by-genotype interaction in the corpus reaching nominal significance —
  a ranking statement.
  - "no such link exists for other redox and metabolic axes" — this discards
  your own best argument. Only one comparator was tested, and redox does link, 
  strongly, in both genotypes (slopes −7.70 / −6.72); it just doesn't link
  differently (interaction p = 0.72). That's a far better control than a null
  axis, because it excludes "the ratio just tracks mitochondrial scores in
  general". Also worth knowing: the pooled OXPHOS correlation is +0.04 — there
  is no main effect at all, the entire signal is the difference of slopes, so 2I
  must never be drawn with a single fitted line.

  Plus: Foxo3 is a failure to rise, not a repression (WT +0.473, Myc+ −0.013,
  and neither genotype half is significant); Foxo3 was not pre-specified — its

  on".
