⏺ First panel is built and verified. Here it is:

  Fig. (unslotted) — the diagonal, decomposed. Ten arms, each row a head-to-tail
  sum: grey = wild-type development, orange = Myc at 12W, black diamond = the
  diagonal. The OXPHOS-subunit row shows the whole argument geometrically — a
  long grey retreat to −0.255, the orange returning it only to +0.061 — and its
  enrichment strip reads 2.73 / 3.28 / 1.27, the one non-bold (non-significant)
  number in the mint block.

  Three things I changed from the plan after seeing the data:

  - The colour bar became printed numbers. Three NES columns plus a bar don't
  fit at 89 mm, and a reader comparing 2.73 with 1.27 is better served by the
  numbers. Fill repeats the value (the "encoded twice" idiom from 1B/2F),
  significance is font weight rather than an asterisk — an asterisk beside a
  number in a 4 mm cell collides with it.
  - The bottom strip is windowed and I've accounted for it. 6 of 143 pathways
  fall outside the shared axis, all on the high side, five with ≤6 genes — the
  membership-loose caveat, and the window understates the compartment's gain.
  Named with gene counts in the legend; nothing falls off the left.
  - A spacer keeps the two x axes aligned. Without it the bottom strip scaled to
  full width and the vertical-drop reading silently broke.

  One correction to what I told you earlier. I said the expression gradient
  "comes from the wild-type timeline, not from Myc." The full table shows that's
  too strong — myc_6W has a gradient as steep as wt_time (Spearman +0.387 vs
  +0.330; Pearson +0.617 vs +0.403). What is true is that myc_12W is weak
  (+0.197, non-monotone across quartiles) and myc_time is flat (+0.069). So the
  honest statement is that the gradient sits in the Myc effect at six weeks and
  in the wild-type timeline, and the null I ran only covers wt_time and cross.
  I'll extend the null to the genotype contrasts in a single amendment at the
  end rather than making you re-run now.

  And the matched null qualifies the headline, which is why the panel doesn't
  say "returns to baseline" anywhere: against 2,000 expression-matched sets the
  OXPHOS subunits still rise on the diagonal, at the 95.4th percentile (null
  median −0.032). They are the lowest mitochondrial arm and the only one that
  doesn't clear its compartment — every other arm sits at ≥99.6. That's a real
  claim; "back to baseline" is not.

  Three things that qualify it

  change" mark appears on any panel.
